## Evidence Cards

### Evidence 64: Higher confidence in GenAI is associated with lower critical-thinking effort.

In a CHI 2025 survey of knowledge workers, confidence in GenAI predicted less self-reported critical-thinking effort, while task-specific self-confidence predicted more critical thinking.

Source: The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking (paper, https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking-self-reported-reductions-in-cognitive-effort-and-confidence-effects-from-a-survey-of-knowledge-workers/)

Limitations: Survey and self-report evidence can reveal associations and lived examples, but it does not prove that GenAI causes durable cognitive decline.

### Evidence 67: Meaningful human oversight should be designed into the AI interface.

The EU AI Act's Article 14 treats oversight as a system design requirement for high-risk AI, including human-machine interface tools that allow natural persons to oversee use.

Source: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 Artificial Intelligence Act - Article 14 Human Oversight (policy, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en)

Limitations: The regulation targets legally defined high-risk AI systems; consumer wellness or learning tools may not be in scope, but the design principle transfers well.

### Evidence 65: GenAI changes critical thinking from creation toward verification and stewardship.

The Microsoft Research paper reports that GenAI-assisted work shifts critical thinking toward checking information, integrating responses, and managing the task rather than only producing original reasoning.

Source: The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking (paper, https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking-self-reported-reductions-in-cognitive-effort-and-confidence-effects-from-a-survey-of-knowledge-workers/)

Limitations: The shift can be productive when tools support verification, but harmful if product design hides sources, uncertainty, or the user's initial reasoning step.

### Evidence 66: Education-facing GenAI needs age-appropriate, human-centred governance.

UNESCO frames GenAI in education around human capacity, data privacy, age limits, ethical validation, and pedagogical design rather than unrestricted answer generation.

Source: Guidance for Generative AI in Education and Research (policy, https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/guidance-generative-ai-education-and-research)

Limitations: Policy guidance is a strong design input, but it should not be treated as experimental proof that a specific interface improves learning outcomes.

### Evidence 68: Digital tools can shift memory from content recall toward access-location recall.

The 2011 Science paper on the Google effect found that expected access to information can reduce recall of the information itself while improving recall of where it can be found.

Source: Google Effects on Memory: Cognitive Consequences of Having Information at Our Fingertips (paper, https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1207745)

Limitations: This predates modern LLMs and search-based behavior is not the same as conversational AI delegation, so it should be used as background theory.

### Evidence 69: Anthropomorphic AI services should detect overdependence and preserve exit paths.

China's interim rules for anthropomorphic AI interaction require risk warnings for overdependence, emotional-boundary guidance, minor protection modes, use-duration reminders, and convenient exit options.

Source: 人工智能拟人化互动服务管理暂行办法 (policy, https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-04/10/c_1777558395078289.htm)

Limitations: This is a regulatory source that identifies governance expectations, not empirical evidence that any specific reminder or limit reduces dependency.

### Evidence 70: K-12 GenAI use should vary by developmental stage and avoid unsupervised open-ended generation for younger students.

The China Education Society guideline recommends boundaries by school stage and says elementary students should not independently use open-ended content-generation functions.

Source: 中小学生成式人工智能使用指南（2025年版） (policy, https://www.cse.edu.cn/index/detail.html?category=31&id=4242)

Limitations: The guideline is highly relevant to education design in China, but implementation outcomes and classroom effects still need primary research and expert review.
